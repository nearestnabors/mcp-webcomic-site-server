# Fran Hopper — Research Notes

## Biography

**Full Name:** Frances R. Deitrick Hopper (née Frances R. Deitrick)
**Born:** July 13, 1922 — Maryland, USA
**Died:** November 29, 2017 — New Jersey, USA (age 95)
**Active Years:** 1943–1948

Fran Hopper was an American comic-book artist active during the 1930s–1940s period known as the Golden Age of Comic Books. She was one of the earliest women to work professionally in the American comic book industry.

Born in Maryland, she grew up in New Jersey with two brothers and two sisters. Her father, Stapleton Conway Deitrick, was a mechanical engineer, and her mother was Emily (Randolph) Deitrick. She studied at the Pratt Institute before entering the comics field.

During World War II, as many male comic artists were drafted into military service, publishers opened their doors to female creators. In 1943, Hopper was hired by the S. M. "Jerry" Iger Studio, one of the era's "packaging studios" that produced outsourced comics for publishers. Editor Ruth Roche recruited her alongside other talented women including Ruth Atkinson, Lily Renée, and Marcia Snyder.

She married Dr. John B. Hopper II in 1944 and began signing her work as "Fran Hopper." After leaving comics around 1948, she raised Arabian horses with her husband in Chester, New Jersey, and continued painting throughout her life. She maintained connections with the comics world, including a friendship with Marvel and Archie artist Al Hartley.

In 2000, she was nominated to the Friends of Lulu Hall of Fame. Comic artist and historian Trina Robbins tracked her down in her retirement home, leading to renewed interest in her career. She was featured in Robbins' anthology *Babes In Arms: Women in the Comics During World War Two* (2017).

## Notable Works

Fran Hopper worked primarily for Fiction House on the following features:

### Planet Comics
- **"Norge Benson"** — Her first confirmed credit (as "Fran Dietrick"), penciler and inker, issues #24–26 (May–Sept 1943)
- **"Gale Allen and the Girl Squadron"** — Issues #28–40 (1944–1945). A science-fiction adventure featuring a female space captain leading an all-woman battalion
- **"Mysta of the Moon"** — Issues #37–40, 42, 48–49 (July 1945 – July 1947). A female scientist with advanced knowledge defending Earth

### Rangers Comics
- **"Private Elmer Pippin and the Colonel's Daughter"** — Humor feature (1943–1944)
- **"Glory Forbes"** — Adventure feature (1945–1946)

### Wings Comics
- **"Jane Martin"** — Espionage series (March 1946 – August 1947). Her final known work
- **"Yank Aces of World War II"** — Biographical feature

### Jungle Comics
- **"Camilla"** — Jungle adventure feature (October 1945 – August 1947)
- **"African Wild Life"** and **"Jungle Facts"** — Nature features

## Historical Significance

Fran Hopper's historical significance lies in several key areas:

1. **Pioneering Female Cartoonist**: She was among the first wave of women to work professionally in American comic books during the Golden Age, paving the way for future generations.

2. **Quality of Work**: Comic historian Trina Robbins noted that Hopper's "art for 'Mysta of the Moon' was often stunning." Planet Comics was considered "at its prime" during the period when she and fellow artist Lily Renée contributed, with critics noting they were "matured and at their best in these series."

3. **Range of Genres**: Unlike many artists who specialized, Hopper demonstrated versatility across science fiction (Planet Comics), humor (Rangers Comics), espionage (Wings Comics), and adventure (Jungle Comics).

4. **All-Female Features**: She illustrated stories featuring strong female protagonists—Gale Allen led an all-woman space battalion, Mysta was a super-scientist, and Jane Martin was a spy—at a time when such portrayals were rare.

## Public Domain Status

**Summary:** Fran Hopper's comic work is in the public domain and freely available for use.

### Legal Basis

Fiction House's comics from the 1940s are in the public domain for multiple reasons:

1. **Pre-1964 Copyright Non-Renewal**: Under the 1909 Copyright Act, works published before 1964 required copyright renewal after 28 years. Fiction House ceased operations in 1954 and did not renew copyrights on most of its catalog.

2. **Corporate Dissolution**: Fiction House went out of business, and no successor organization maintained copyright claims on these works.

3. **Public Domain Archives**: Multiple reputable public domain archives (Digital Comic Museum, Comic Book Plus) host Fiction House comics, confirming their public domain status after decades of availability.

### Verification

The public domain status is confirmed by:
- Inclusion in the Digital Comic Museum's collection
- Availability on Comic Book Plus, whose mission is to host only public domain content
- Academic and commercial reprints (PS Artbooks, Gwandanaland Comics) that explicitly cite public domain status
- Listing on the Public Domain Super Heroes Wiki
